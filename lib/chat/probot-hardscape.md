## How to tell the jobs apart

- Concrete pavers are cast units set on a compacted base and bedding sand, with sand in the joints. A new patio, walkway, or driveway is an installation. Loose, rocking, or sunken units are a repair. A dull, faded, or sand-losing surface may only need cleaning and sealing.
- Travertine is natural stone. It is often chosen for pool decks because the surface stays more comfortable in the sun than many darker pavers. It is a different install from concrete pavers. Do not treat “pavers” and “travertine” as the same product.
- Porcelain pavers are a manufactured tile, usually denser and less porous than travertine. Homeowners ask if they are durable and what a porcelain patio costs. Explain the difference. Do not invent the cost.
- A seat wall or low decorative wall is not a retaining wall. A retaining wall or tall block wall holds back soil. In the Las Vegas valley, a wall around 4 feet and taller usually needs a permit and engineering. Tell the visitor a pro has to confirm the current Clark County rule. Do not design the wall.
- Coping is the cap on a pool edge. It is its own job, not the same as the deck field.
- Drainage and irrigation are separate from the surface. A patio that holds water, a washout after rain, or a broken drip or sprinkler line is a drainage or irrigation question even when pavers are also involved.
- Artificial turf and desert rock or plants solve different problems. Turf is a surface. Desert planting, rock, and drip irrigation are the landscape around it. Yard cleanup and ongoing maintenance are visits, not an installation.

## Las Vegas conditions

- Summer heat is extreme. Light-colored stone and a surface made for bare feet matter on pool decks and south-facing patios. Dark surfaces get hotter.
- Monsoon rain shows weak spots. Pavers that sink after rain usually have a failed base or bedding, or water with nowhere to go. Replacing only the top unit often fails again. Polymeric joint sand washes out when water sits or the sand was not set correctly.
- White haze on pavers or block is often efflorescence, mineral salts moving out of the material. It is common. It is not automatically a failed install.
- Valley ground often has caliche, a hard mineral layer. It changes excavation, drainage, and how deep a base can go. A pro has to see the yard.
- Many neighborhoods, including master-planned areas in Summerlin, Henderson, and North Las Vegas, have an HOA. Color, height, and plant lists may need approval before work starts.
- Natural lawns are a poor default in the desert. Drip irrigation and desert-adapted plants fit the climate. Artificial turf is common where someone wants a green surface without a lawn. Do not invent a water-authority rebate amount.
- Sealing protects concrete pavers and some stone. The surface has to be clean and dry. Sealing does not fix a sinking base.

## What to tell a homeowner to ask a pro

- How the area is measured, and whether old concrete or old pavers have to come out.
- What the base is, where water goes, and who handles an HOA or a permit.
- What is included: labor, materials, joint sand, sealer, and haul-away.
- Warranty on labor and on materials, as two different things.
